01 /

The setup toolkit

From finding your Steam library to preserving your launch options, the small details are covered.

Finds your game

Detects Steam and Project Zomboid across configured libraries, including secondary drives.

Connects the launchers

Updates Steam Launch Options and the game’s JSON, batch or shell launchers for your platform.

Keeps a way back

Preserves original backups and unrelated settings, with rollback and recovery when an installation fails.

Places the mod files

Copies the native library and ZombieBuddy.jar supplied by your selected Workshop branch.

Two branches, one tool

Choose LTS or Beta, reinstall, change branches or remove ZombieBuddy from the installer.

Shows its work

Open diagnostics with F5 to check paths, Workshop state, launcher files and installation logs.

From download to ready

Install Steam and Project Zomboid first. Close the game, open the tool and choose your build.

01

Find your files

The installer reads Steam’s libraries and locates the game and Workshop branch.

02

Prepare a snapshot

Steam closes gracefully, then the installer records the files it will change.

03

Install and patch

The native library and JAR go into place, and the launch settings are updated.

04

Check the result

Follow the status in the app. Steam restarts after a successful installation.

Choose the right download

  • Windows x64 · EXE · WebView2 required
  • Linux x64 · Experimental · WebKitGTK 4.1 and desktop dependencies; run chmod +x on the downloaded file
  • macOS Apple Silicon · Experimental · Unzip and open the APP; Intel Macs are not included

Before you open it

The applications are unsigned. Windows SmartScreen or macOS Gatekeeper may show a warning.

New Rust builds are prepared as draft candidates. The latest public release may still contain the legacy Go application. Check its release notes for the files and requirements supplied.
